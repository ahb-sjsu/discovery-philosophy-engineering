\begin{abstract}
Philosophy is rich in claims about which distinctions matter, which descriptions
are equivalent, what counts as rational or objective, and which structures are
fundamental. Many such claims stay insulated from empirical pressure because they
are stated at a level too informal to generate a discriminating test. That
invariance under admissible transformation marks what is real rather than how we
happened to describe it is an old idea with four developed statements already, in
the study of what a transformation group leaves fixed, in robustness as agreement
across independent means of detection, in invariance under intervention as the
mark of a causal relation, and in the structure that survives the replacement of
one theory by another. This paper does not propose a fifth. It proposes that the
idea be made to cost something, by compiling a philosophical claim into an
executable invariance test, declaring the admissible class in advance as a
versioned artifact, reducing each failure to the smallest transformation that
produces it, naming the component that absorbs the change, and recording the
revision that follows. The result is a discipline we call Discovery Philosophy
Engineering, in which an implementation is not the endpoint of philosophical
analysis but an instrument for experimenting on it. We define an invariance
envelope as the family of transformations under which a claim survives, a boundary
witness as the smallest admissible transformation that breaks it, and a
comparative notion of fundamentality that ranks two descriptions of comparable
adequacy by their envelopes. We then report the discipline running. A campaign
graded four of its own theory's predictions through registrations sealed before
the data were read, returning one pass whose interesting number is the half of the
population that violates the law, two indeterminate verdicts whose reduction
located where a theorem stops applying, and one pass in which two of three
declared budgets behaved as the theory said and the third did not. A discovery
line asked one question nine times, failed three of the nine by clauses written in
advance, and ended with one law, one declared scope, and two recorded boundaries.
The check the discipline prescribes then found a defect in its own authors'
records that reading them had not. What is new here is not a criterion of
invariance. It is the accounting that lets somebody else test one.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} invariance, robustness, objectivity, scientific
discovery, representation independence, theory revision, fundamentality,
preregistration, computational philosophy

\section{Introduction}

Philosophical inquiry repeatedly asks questions of one form. Which distinctions
are real? Which descriptions are different presentations of the same case? Which
aspects of a judgment are objective, and which depend on a standpoint? What is
preserved when a problem is translated, reparameterized, rescaled, or embedded in
a richer representation? And when two theories disagree, which disagreement
reflects the world and which reflects the language in which the world was
described?

These are questions about invariance, and philosophy has answered them in that
vocabulary for a long time. Section 2 sets out four developed answers, because the
argument here depends on conceding how much of its central idea is already held.
What this paper adds is not another account of why invariance matters. It is a way
of making a particular invariance claim cost something to assert.

The difficulty is not that philosophers lack a criterion. It is that a criterion
stated in prose leaves three things in the claimant's hands and shows the reader
none of them. The class of transformations said to be irrelevant is chosen by the
theorist, and a class chosen after the result is a class fitted to it. A failure is
reported as a failure, without the smaller failure inside it that would say which
assumption gave way. A commitment revised in the light of a counterexample is
revised silently, so the record cannot separate a theory that learned from a theory
that was never wrong. None of these is a defect of the invariance idea. Each is a
defect of the accounting around it.

Experimental philosophy has subjected philosophical judgments to empirical study
and found both stability and fragility in methods of cases
\citep{knobe2024,irikefe2025}. Computational philosophy uses mechanized methods to
extend philosophical analysis \citep{thagard2024}. Formal philosophy turns
conceptual commitments into mathematical structures. Automated scientific discovery
searches data for compact laws, increasingly through symbolic regression and
closed-loop systems \citep{makke2024,kramer2026}. Each supplies a necessary piece.
None supplies a discipline for taking one philosophical proposition, compiling it
into invariance and boundary claims, declaring in advance what would refute it,
attacking it, and recording what the attack did to it in a form a third party can
check.

This paper proposes that discipline, which we call Discovery Philosophy
Engineering. It extends Philosophy Engineering, a method for operationalizing
philosophical commitments through declared equivalences, invariance requirements,
enforcement mechanisms, and machine-checkable artifacts \citep{bond2026pe}. The
extension closes the loop. Rather than moving only from a philosophical principle
to a formal constraint to an implementation, the implementation is turned back on
the principle as an instrument. If a proposition can be formalized well enough to
implement, the implementation usually permits experiments on it that argument alone
cannot perform.

The contribution is methodological. Many philosophical questions are conceptual,
normative, or modal in ways no test settles, and the discipline claims nothing
about those. It applies where a philosophical claim entails observable invariance,
representation, discrimination, or boundary structure, and its aim is to sharpen
those entailments until success and failure are both informative.

\section{Invariance has been asked to do this work before}

The thought that what survives admissible transformation is a better candidate for
the real than what does not is old, and it has at least four developed statements.
A proposal in this area earns attention only by saying what it adds to them.

\textbf{Geometry as the study of what a group leaves fixed.} The Erlangen reading
classifies geometries by the transformation groups whose invariants they study
\citep{klein1893}, and the extension of that reading to physical law is
\citet{weyl1952}. This is the origin of the move and remains its clearest case.
What it supplies is the form of the question. What it does not supply is guidance
when the admissible group is not handed over by the subject matter, which is the
normal situation outside mathematics and physics.

\textbf{Objectivity as invariance.} \citet{nozick2001} makes invariance under
admissible transformations the account of objectivity itself, so that an objective
fact is one holding across the transformations that do not matter. This is the
closest existing statement of the present thesis, and Definition 3 below is a near
relative of it. Nozick offers an analysis of a concept. He does not offer a
procedure for deciding a particular case, and the question of who fixes the
admissible transformations is raised rather than answered.

\textbf{Robustness as agreement across independent means of detection.}
\citet{levins1966} defended robustness analysis in model building and
\citet{wimsatt1981} generalized it, using the word robust for exactly what is
invariant across independent means of determination. This is the structure of the
invariance envelope of Section 4.3, reached three decades earlier and from the
practice of working scientists rather than from geometry. The difference is
bookkeeping rather than concept. Robustness analysis reports that a result held
across several independent routes. It does not generally declare the set of routes
in advance, does not require failures to be reduced, and has no standard form in
which a reader can see which routes were tried and rejected. The literature knows
this, and the charge that robustness can be manufactured by choosing agreeable
models is the standing objection to it.

\textbf{Invariance under intervention.} \citet{woodward2003} makes invariance under
a range of interventions the mark separating a causal generalization from an
accidental one, and treats invariance explicitly as a matter of degree and range
rather than as a predicate. The degree and range treatment is the move made here
with the envelope, and Woodward's account is the more developed of the two as
philosophy. What it is not is a procedure with an audit trail, since the range over
which a relation is invariant is established by argument and by experiment reported
in the ordinary way.

\textbf{Structure as what survives theory change.} Structural realism holds that
what carries across a scientific revolution is mathematical structure rather than
ontology \citep{worrall1989}. Replace the transformation from one theory to its
successor by a transformation from one representation to another and the
resemblance is close. \citet{ismael2003} sharpen the related point that a symmetry
marks superfluous theoretical structure, which is the philosophical form of the
factorization criterion stated as Proposition 1 below. Their argument and that
proposition say one thing in two vocabularies.

The concession is therefore large. The criterion is not new, the treatment of
invariance as a matter of degree is not new, the use of a symmetry to identify
structure that can be quotiented away is not new, and the identification of
objectivity with invariance is not new. Five of this paper's central ideas have
owners.

What none of them supplies is an account under which an invariance claim can be
checked by somebody who does not trust the claimant. In each, the admissible class
is settled inside the argument that uses it. That is not a criticism of the
authors, because prose was the medium they had. It is a description of what is left
to be added. The proposal here is that the three things a prose statement leaves
implicit, the class, the reduction of the failure, and the revision, become
artifacts declared before the test, fixed by content hash, and refused by a checker
when absent. \citet{mayo2018} supplies the notion that makes this worth doing,
since a test is severe when the claim would probably have failed it had the claim
been false, and severity is exactly what a class chosen after the fact destroys.

The novelty claimed here is accordingly narrow. It is not a new criterion of
invariance. It is the accounting that lets an old one be operated by more than one
person.

\section{A claim becomes an instrument when its equivalences are declared}

Philosophy Engineering was introduced as the systematic translation of epistemic,
ethical, and logical commitments into computationally enforceable constraints on
autonomous systems \citep{bond2026pe}. Its procedure has four steps. Declare a
domain-specific equivalence relation over representations. Declare what must remain
invariant. Implement canonicalization, quotienting, equivariance, or another
enforcement mechanism. Package transformation trials and witnesses as
machine-checkable artifacts. Its central example is the Epistemic Invariance
Principle, which holds that a judgment procedure should not change its conclusion
under transformations declared to preserve task-relevant meaning.

Discovery Philosophy Engineering generalizes this in the reverse direction. A
philosophical claim becomes a source of formal commitments that a transformation
grammar and an observation process can interrogate. The engineering artifact stops
being the endpoint of analysis and becomes an instrument for experimenting on it.

\begin{principle}[Discovery Philosophy Engineering]
A philosophical conjecture is a candidate for Discovery Philosophy Engineering when
it can be translated into at least one non-trivial formal constraint whose
satisfaction or violation is observable under a declared family of transformations,
interventions, or representation changes.
\end{principle}

Non-triviality is essential. A constant judgment rule is invariant under every
transformation and has learned nothing. Philosophy Engineering therefore pairs
invariance with discriminative adequacy, so that irrelevant differences must not
matter and relevant differences must be allowed to matter, and the present
discipline inherits that dual obligation. Figure~\ref{fig:loop} shows the loop.

\section{Formal architecture}

\subsection{Representations, transformations, and judgments}

Let $S$ denote a space of world states or cases, $X$ a representation space, and
$\sigma : S \to X$ an encoding map. Let $Y$ be an output space, and let a judgment
or theory-induced prediction be
\begin{equation}
J : X \to \mathcal{P}(Y),
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal{P}(Y)$ is the space of distributions over $Y$ and a deterministic
judgment is a degenerate distribution. Let $\mathcal{T}$ be a set of transformations
$\tau : X \to X$ declared to preserve some target-relevant structure.

The declaration matters, and it is where this architecture departs from the
accounts of Section 2. No method discovers relevance without assumptions. An
admissible transformation is not anything that can be done to the data. It is a
transformation licensed by the philosophical claim under test. Unit conversion,
variable renaming, permutation of independent premises, paraphrase preserving
formal denotation, change of basis, population transfer, and stake rescaling are
different transformations with different semantics, and a claim surviving one says
nothing about another.

\begin{definition}[Invariance claim]
Given an output equivalence relation $\sim_Y$, the judgment $J$ is invariant under
$\mathcal{T}$ if
\begin{equation}
J(x) \sim_Y J(\tau x) \qquad \forall x \in X, \ \tau \in \langle \mathcal{T} \rangle,
\end{equation}
where $\langle \mathcal{T} \rangle$ is the closure generated by $\mathcal{T}$ when
composition is meaningful.
\end{definition}

This is the formal core of the Epistemic Invariance Principle \citep{bond2026pe}.
Read as a compliance property it is a specification. Read as this paper reads it,
it is an empirically attackable consequence of the philosophical commitment that
the transformations in $\mathcal{T}$ do not matter.

\subsection{A theory calling a difference representational should factor through the quotient}

Let $x \sim_{\mathcal{T}} x'$ when two representations lie in the same orbit under
the admissible transformations. The quotient $X / \sim_{\mathcal{T}}$ identifies
every description the theory says is the same case.

\begin{proposition}[Factorization criterion]
If $J$ is constant on every $\sim_{\mathcal{T}}$ equivalence class, then there
exists a well-defined map
$\bar{J} : X/\sim_{\mathcal{T}} \to \mathcal{P}(Y)$ such that
$J = \bar{J} \circ q$, where $q : X \to X/\sim_{\mathcal{T}}$ is the quotient map.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Define $\bar{J}([x]) = J(x)$. Class constancy makes the definition independent of
the representative chosen. Then $\bar{J}(q(x)) = J(x)$ by construction.
\end{proof}

The mathematics is elementary and the reading carries the weight. A theory calling
certain differences merely representational is committed to a judgment factoring
through the quotient that removes them, and a failure to factor locates a
representational dependence the theory did not admit to having. This is the content
of the argument that a symmetry marks superfluous theoretical structure
\citep{ismael2003}, stated so a program can test it on a particular case.

\subsection{Invariance is an envelope rather than a predicate}

Pass and fail are too coarse for discovery. A theory may be invariant under unit
changes and not under scale changes, stable across paraphrases and not across
languages, transferable to low-stakes contexts and not to consequential ones.

\begin{definition}[Invariance envelope]
For a theory or judgment procedure $J$ and a universe $U$ of candidate
transformations, the invariance envelope is
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{E}(J) = \{\, \tau \in U \ : \ J(x) \sim_Y J(\tau x)
\ \text{over the declared test domain} \,\}.
\end{equation}
\end{definition}

The test domain must be reported, because an empirical envelope is always relative
to sampled cases, measurement tolerance, and statistical power. The formal object
is an idealization and empirical work estimates it. Treating invariance as a matter
of degree and range is \citeauthor{woodward2003}'s move
\citeyearpar{woodward2003}, and the set of routes across which a result holds is
\citeauthor{wimsatt1981}'s \citeyearpar{wimsatt1981}. The envelope adds one
requirement to both, that $U$ be written down before the test rather than recovered
from it afterwards.

\begin{definition}[Comparative fundamentality]
Let $J_1$ and $J_2$ be candidate descriptions with comparable empirical adequacy
and without compensating increases in unconstrained complexity. Then $J_1$ is more
invariantly fundamental than $J_2$ relative to $U$ if
$\mathcal{E}(J_2) \subseteq \mathcal{E}(J_1)$.
\end{definition}

This is comparative and local by design. It does not identify metaphysical
fundamentality with invariance, and it is not offered as a contribution to the
metaphysics of fundamentality, where the relations at issue are grounding and
ontological priority rather than survival under redescription. It says only that
when two descriptions account for the same phenomena, the one surviving more
meaning-preserving changes has discarded more representational contingency. A
reader who holds \citeauthor{nozick2001}'s \citeyearpar{nozick2001} account of
objectivity may read the definition as that account restricted to a declared $U$
and made decidable for a particular pair.

\begin{remark}
The complexity qualification blocks a vacuous victory in which invariance is bought
by adding a correction for every transformation. The discipline therefore pairs
envelope breadth with parsimony, preregistration, and prospective prediction.
Without the qualification, comparative fundamentality would reward exactly the ad
hoc manoeuvre the robustness literature identifies as its own main risk.
\end{remark}

\subsection{A failure says more when it is reduced to its smallest witness}

A radar return is useful because it localizes. The analogue for theories is a
counterexample small enough to name a cause.

\begin{definition}[Boundary witness]
A boundary witness for an invariance claim is a transformation or intervention
$\tau^\star$ such that $J(x) \not\sim_Y J(\tau^\star x)$ for some $x$, while no
strictly simpler admissible predecessor under a declared complexity ordering
produces the violation.
\end{definition}

For a finite transformation grammar, breadth-first search identifies a minimal
violating composition under word length \citep{bond2026pe}. The move is standard in
software debugging, where delta debugging narrows a failing input to a minimal
failure-inducing core \citep{zeller2002}, and the methodological content is
identical. A reduced failure names a component and an unreduced one does not. For
continuous parameters the boundary is estimated as
\begin{equation}
t^\star = \inf \{\, t \ : \ D\big(J(x), J(T_t x)\big) > \epsilon \,\},
\label{eq:boundary}
\end{equation}
with $D$ an output discrepancy and $\epsilon$ a tolerance fixed in advance. A
failed invariance test then measures where a degree of freedom supposed to be
irrelevant starts to matter.

\section{The protocol}

The discipline organizes work as a sequence of increasingly severe tests, severe in
\citeauthor{mayo2018}'s sense \citeyearpar{mayo2018}. The objective is not to
maximize the phenomena a theory can be made to fit after the fact. It is to find
the smallest structure that still predicts after everything the theory says should
not matter has been changed.

\subsection{Formalize the commitment}

Replace broad language, such as objective, rational, same case, irrelevant framing,
and fundamental, with explicit mathematical commitments. These may be equivalence
relations, group actions, order restrictions, metric identities, conservation
conditions, commutative diagrams, or conditional independences. Formalization must
separate four statuses, which are definitions, modelling assumptions, derived
propositions, and empirical hypotheses. The common error in interdisciplinary work
is treating a convenient representation as an observed law, and the separation
prevents it.

\subsection{Derive consequences that divide rivals}

A good probe is not one under which the theory could fail. It is one under which
competing theories diverge. Candidate consequences should be ranked by expected
discriminatory value, and invariance where rivals predict sensitivity, sign
reversals, boundary locations, low-rank constraints, monotonicity, and forbidden
orderings are the useful kinds. The strongest tests are often consequences the
theorist would not have chosen, and they are valuable because the formal structure
forces them.

\subsection{Search for low-complexity structure}

The discovery engine may be human reasoning, symbolic regression, equation
discovery, structure learning, or a hybrid. Symbolic regression is attractive
because it searches for concise relations rather than only for predictive black
boxes \citep{makke2024}, recent work applies it to interpretable model discovery
under physical constraints \citep{hou2024}, and broader automated-science systems
combine representation learning, equation discovery, experiment selection, and
closed-loop autonomy \citep{kramer2026}. A search objective can be written
\begin{equation}
\min_{F \in \mathcal{H}} \ L(F) + \lambda\, C(F) + \mu\, I(F),
\end{equation}
with $L$ a predictive loss, $C$ a structural complexity, and $I$ a penalty on
instability under transformations the programme has declared irrelevant. This is
not proposed as a unique objective. It states the preference for descriptions that
are accurate, simple, and invariant together.

\subsection{Attack the representation}

Subject a candidate law to transformations preserving information while changing
coordinates or presentation. Basis rotations, unit changes, equivalent encodings,
feature permutations, redundant embeddings, alternative encoders, and controlled
compressions are the usual instruments. Structure disappearing under an
information-preserving change of coordinates may belong to the representation
rather than to the phenomenon. Contemporary symmetry discovery does the
corresponding technical work, enforcing known symmetries, searching for unknown
ones, and breaking candidate symmetries only where data require it
\citep{otto2025}, and causal representation learning has been reframed so
identifiability runs through preserved invariances rather than one fixed formalism
\citep{yao2025}. The philosophical reading is that a distinction should be kept
only if the world forces us to keep it.

\subsection{Freeze, then probe what was not used to build it}

After discovery, freeze parameters and transformation rules as far as possible and
evaluate on unseen cases, populations, scales, or domains. This is where a
suggestive reduction becomes evidence of transferable structure.

\subsection{Reduce failures rather than hide them}

A null result may be uninformative. A localized contradiction is not. When an
invariance fails, find the smallest witness and ask which component absorbs the
change, the candidates being the representation, the metric, the constraints, the
resolution budget, the dynamics, and the philosophical equivalence relation itself.
This turns falsification from a verdict into cartography. The aim is not to
immunize a theory against failure but to make its failures diagnostic.

\section{Where this sits among existing methods}

Experimental philosophy studies the distribution and stability of judgments
elicited by cases, and recent work continues to debate calibration, comprehension,
and robustness to error \citep{knobe2024,irikefe2025,cova2025}. The present
discipline shares the commitment that philosophical practice can be interrogated
empirically and differs in its unit of analysis, targeting formal consequences of
philosophical theories rather than primarily the psychology of philosophers or lay
participants. Human judgments may be the dependent variable, and so may physical
measurements, algorithmic behaviour, economic choices, or theorem-prover outcomes.

Computational philosophy uses simulation, automated reasoning, and formal modelling
to instantiate and extend philosophical research \citep{thagard2024}, and this is a
narrower proposal inside that space, distinguished by the closed loop from
conjecture to executable test to reduced witness to revision. Formal philosophy
supplies the language without which the proposal would be metaphor, and the
discipline adds the requirement that a formal claim with observable consequences
also specify how those consequences are tested and what would count as a minimal
counterexample. Automated scientific discovery has moved from equation discovery
toward systems selecting experiments and learning representations
\citep{kramer2026}, and the difference here is the type of prior knowledge
compiled, since the starting commitments are philosophical and part of the goal is
to find which of them survive contact with the world.

\section{The discipline at work}

Five instances follow. The first is generic. The next three are reported briefly,
each making a single point. The fifth is reported in full, because it is the only
case in which the protocol of Section 5 ran to its own declared end. The sixth is
the case in which the discipline's own check found a defect in the records of the
programme that wrote it.

Every number in Sections 7.2, 7.5, and 7.6 is read from a sealed record in a public
repository, cited where it appears. None is restated from a draft.

\subsection{Epistemic invariance as an experimental object}

The Epistemic Invariance Principle says a judgment should remain equivalent under
meaning-preserving transformations \citep{bond2026pe}. In its engineering role it
is a specification, so a system changing a medical recommendation because
laboratory results were reordered violates a declared norm. The discovery role is
different.

Suppose a class $\mathcal{T}$ is proposed as semantically irrelevant. An engineer
can build the transformation suite, measure judgment discrepancy, and search for
minimal witnesses. Three outcomes are possible. Invariance may hold broadly, and
the claim that the transformed inputs are the same case gains operational support.
Invariance may fail because the model is representation-sensitive while the
philosophical equivalence remains compelling, in which case the system is defective
against the norm. Or invariance may fail systematically in a way showing the
proposed equivalence erased a relevant distinction, in which case the philosophy and
not only the implementation should change. The third outcome is the distinctive
one, and it is where engineering exposes a hidden philosophical assumption by
forcing it to meet a concrete counterexample.

\subsection{A campaign that graded four of its own predictions}

Geometric Evaluation Theory is an evaluator-relative theory in which states and
actions map into a consequence space, an evaluator supplies a metric and an ideal
point, a finite budget limits the distinctions the evaluator can make, and
preference and choice follow from those distinctions \citep{bond2026get}. Its draft
separates what the world supplies, the states, the actions, and the consequence
map, from what the evaluator owns, the metric, the budget, and the admissible set.
Every claim is labelled proved, defined, posited, or open, and predictions are
graded through gates sealed before the data are read. Four of its empirical
questions have run across five sealed registrations.

The first tested the hull law, a consequence of the representation theorem that no
action is strictly worse than every action whose consequences surround it. The
consequence map must be known, so the world was the 1972 American National Election
Study, in which respondents placed candidates and parties on seven-point issue
scales and rated them on feeling thermometers. The registration was sealed before
any rating was read. On the registered pair of scales 489 respondents had a menu in
which the law could bind, 50 percent violated it at least once, against 66 percent
when each respondent's ratings were shuffled among that respondent's own objects,
and no shuffle in 200 reached the observed rate. Five other pairs of scales gave
the same ordering. The verdict by the sealed bars was a pass, and the number that
matters more than the verdict is the half. The theorem is deterministic for a
representable preference, so the law holds in this population as a tendency and
fails as a law for half the respondents. In the vocabulary of Section 4.4 the
population is a boundary witness against the deterministic reading, and what
absorbs it is not yet registered, since no error model for integer placements and
integer ratings was part of the seal.

The second tested the claim that the indifference threshold of the induced order is
the evaluator's resolution budget. The evaluator was a language model asked how far
a value sits from a target and answering with a number, and three budgets were set
independently, the decimals shown, the tokens allowed for the report, and the
precision of the weights. A cell's threshold is the separation at which the scorer orders
90 percent of pairs correctly. On fresh pairs every graded threshold sat at 0.87 to
0.88 of the step the rendering imposed and of the resolution the report length
afforded, the largest ratio 1.15 against a tolerance of 1.5 fixed before the seal,
and the constant near 0.87 is where the 90 percent level of a rounding scorer
falls, as a self-test on a known grid had shown. The weight precision did not move
the threshold at all, eight-bit and four-bit weights leaving it where full
precision put it to the third digit. Two of three declared budgets are resolution
budgets and the third is not, which is the finding, and the registration could have
carried only the two that worked. Two instruments tried before the one that ran are
kept in the record rather than dropped. A pairwise chooser read from answer-letter
logits was vacuous when the options straddled the target, because the model picks
the larger number, and had a floor of 7.5 when they did not, from position bias.
The reduction of that failure generalizes past the case. A pairwise chooser
measures its own position bias before it measures a threshold.

The third tested the incompatibility theorem, which says several evaluators reading
one rank-limited representation each pay a regret fixed by their geometries, that a
shared representation best for all exists only when their leading subspaces
coincide, and that least total regret is attained by the leading eigenspace of the
summed geometries. The world was the grouped-query attention of a language model,
with three query heads as evaluators of one key cache and shared codes of rank 2,
4, and 8 in 128 dimensions. The first sealed run coded all keys at once and found
the measured attention loss 20 to 330 times the second-order prediction, so the
regret formula was not tested and the verdict was indeterminate. The ordering claim
survived, the leading eigenspace of the summed geometries beating every random code
and the key-covariance code in 32 of 32 cases, while a head's own leading eigenspace
was best for that head in fewer than half. The failure was then reduced, as Section
5.6 requires. The registered prediction and the registered measurement were not the
same object, a one-key operator against an all-keys loss, and the codes sat far
outside the range where the quadratic approximation holds. A second sealed run
measured the object the formula
predicts, one key perturbed by a small isotropic error in the discarded subspace,
along a ladder of perturbation sizes. The ratio of measured to predicted loss was
flat in the perturbation size up to a tenth of a whitened unit with median over
cells 1.02 to 1.05, so the recovered operators predict the loss without bias, and 64
draws per cell left a 20 percent scatter that missed the registered per-triple bar,
so that verdict was indeterminate as well. A seed check with fresh draws showed the
scatter to be draw noise. Two errors in that registration's own error estimate are
recorded in it. What the two runs establish together is the boundary of the
theorem's range of validity. Inside it the regret formula and the bound follow from
the
recovered operators, so the only empirical content is whether the loss is quadratic
with the recovered operator, which holds. Outside it the ordering survives and the
private optimum does not. The perturbation ladder is
equation~\eqref{eq:boundary} executed.

The fourth tested identification, the theorem that an evaluator's choices recover
the metric up to scale and the ideal up to the metric's kernel, with the design
bound that the consequences must span the space affinely. The estimator is the
representation theorem's semidefinite program. Two pilots before the seal caught
two wrong drafts of that estimator and showed the drafted absolute tolerances were
empty, since identification from ordinal data is slow, so the bars were restated on
medians relative to each evaluator's chance level. The sealed run passed in all
twelve cells on a fresh seed. From a weak order on 64 consequences above the
dimension, the metric and the ideal's range component were recovered to a few
percent of chance with error falling at every step of the battery ladder. At or
below the design bound, off the span of a battery confined to a subspace, and in
the kernel of a singular metric, nothing was recovered. A semiorder withholding the
fifth of pairs closest in distance identified more slowly. The negative claims, what
choices cannot reveal, came out as sharp as the positive one, and each would have
been a witness against the theorem or its remark had it failed.

This theory suits the discipline because it makes representation choice explicit.
Under an invertible change of basis $z' = Az$ a quadratic metric transforms as
\begin{equation}
G' = A^{-T} G A^{-1},
\end{equation}
so that
\begin{equation}
z'^{T} G' z' = z^{T} G z ,
\end{equation}
and the evaluative distance is invariant while the coordinates are not. Which
properties of evaluation survive changes of representation, encoder,
dimensionality, and task is then the question the campaign asks gate by gate. The
campaign file, the ledger, the sealed registrations, the run logs, and every result
file are public.

\subsection{A published invariance that failed on independent data}

A geometric model of economic behaviour accepted by a systems journal contains both
a successful reduction and an independent failure \citep{bond2026tcss}. It
represents alternatives in a nine-dimensional decision space and fits a diagonal
Mahalanobis metric. Structural search selects social impact, virtue and identity,
and epistemic status as active dimensions on the reported benchmark, and the
monetary dimension is inactive in the selected low-stakes calibration.

The methodological step is not the fit but the consequence the inactive monetary
coordinate forces. Under the share-normalized encoding, positive rescaling of all
monetary stakes should leave predicted offers and rejections unchanged, which is a
sharp stake-scaling invariance. Tested on individual-level high-stakes ultimatum
data that played no part in fitting or selection, offers and rejection rates changed
substantially with stake, and stake retained predictive power after conditioning on
offer share. The invariance fails.

Under a confirmatory reading this is a negative result. Under the present one it is
a boundary measurement, because the contradiction identifies a specific missing
quantity, share normalization having removed consequential monetary magnitude. An
income-relative monetary coordinate is then a new testable hypothesis, that
decisions depend on stake relative to local income rather than on nominal stake. The
route runs from geometry to invariance to independent probe to boundary witness to a
new representation. A radar return is not a failure. It is information about where
the theory stops describing the terrain.

\subsection{Symbolic search that reports when it loses}

Theory Radar discovers compact classifiers from tabular data and evaluates whether
they can fairly replace ensemble models on the dataset at hand
\citep{bond2026radar}, combining formula enumeration, search acceleration,
representation augmenters, and held-out replay. Its stated value lies in the
comparison rather than in symbolic formulas winning, and some datasets admit compact
formulas matching or beating ensembles while others do not.

The methodological relevance is that it operationalizes a preference for
low-complexity explanation without treating simplicity as sacred, since a formula is
accepted only if it survives fair held-out evaluation. Where the boundary is
intrinsically high-dimensional the ensemble wins, and that negative result is
informative because it identifies a domain in which compression to the searched
grammar loses predictive structure. The natural extension scores candidates for
stability under admissible representation changes as well as for accuracy and
syntactic size, which turns symbolic search into a way of finding simple formulas
whose content survives transformations that should not matter.

\subsection{Nine registrations and one law}
\label{sec:od}

The cases above each exercise part of the loop. The following line exercises all of
it, nine times, under sealed registrations, and is the only case on record in which
the protocol ran to its declared end \citep{bond2026odline}. It also supplies what
the others do not, a measurement of what the protocol costs. Three of the nine
registrations failed.

A hub is a row of a corpus appearing in many other rows' nearest-neighbour lists.
The instrument counts, for every row, how often it is retrieved among the ten
nearest neighbours of the others. The Poisson hub excess is the busiest row's count
divided by the largest count a random assignment of the same retrieval slots would
reach, so an excess of one is chance. The observer is a linear read, a rotation
followed by a diagonal rescaling whose spectrum falls as a power of the index, the
exponent running from zero, a pure rotation, to two, a reader that sees one
direction. Earlier gates had shown that a corpus's hubs are made by the reader as
much as by the corpus. The question was whether the amount of hubness is lawful in
the reader's own variables.

The family of laws was declared once and kept. A law is linear in at most three
features drawn from a fixed pool of transforms of variables measured on the
observed data. The search fits every model of the family on training worlds and
ranks by error on held-out worlds under unseen observer spectra, the pilot freezes
the best, the registration is sealed by renaming the file with the frozen law inside
it, the run uses a fresh seed, and a sealed grader grades. Every bar is a multiple
of the frozen law's own pooled pilot error, fixed before the seal, and a competitor
beating the law pooled on the transfer groups is a failure by a clause written in
advance.

The first four registrations changed the discovery set and kept everything else, and
the law changed with the set each time, which is the finding. A law found on
Gaussian clouds read the distance concentration and the spectral dimension and
failed off Gaussians. A law found on seven shape families held every synthetic
family and the SIFT descriptors and missed the Wikipedia embeddings, whose intrinsic
dimension of 8 to 20 sat under a spectral dimension of up to 320. The intrinsic
dimension was in the pool and the search had dropped it, because on full-dimensional
families it duplicates the spectral dimension. Putting manifold worlds into the set
made the search take it, and the Wikipedia error fell from 5.3 to 2.5 to 1.0 across
the three gates. The discovery set decides the law.

The next two attacked the family rather than the set and both found the same limit.
A pool extended with four scale-free tail measures of the neighbour distances was
offered to the search, and the search declined every one of them, an outcome the
registration had named in advance as its first falsification condition. Placing the
heavy tails in the discovery set at the dimensions the transfer asked about then
bought the heavy tails and lost the high-dimensional clouds, the previously frozen
law was the better predictor pooled, and the gate failed by its own clause. Three
registrations had by then put the same worlds on both sides of one limit, the
largest error of every law sitting where the hubness skewness exceeds 12, where a
law linear in any transform of the skewness cannot turn over. The record said the
family, and not the set, was the limit.

The seventh changed the target and nothing else, predicting the log of the Poisson
hub excess in place of the skewness. On the same 73 worlds and readers the search
froze a law in the concentration of the tenth-neighbour distance, the spectral
dimension, and the intrinsic dimension, with pooled pilot error 0.328, a factor of
1.39 in the excess, against 1.135 for the nominal formula and 1.598 for chance. On
the run it held the heavy tails, the Gaussian at 512 dimensions, four fresh real
slices, and the largest samples at once, and it beat the nominal formula and chance
pooled and inside every group, the first law of the line to do so. Same family,
same pool, same search, same worlds. The target had been the limit all along.

The eighth asked whether the new target takes coverage without paying for it, and it
does not. Adding the highest-dimensional clouds to the training set bought every
full-dimensional world beyond 256 dimensions and the best real-slice errors of the
line, and paid on every heavy-tailed world and on an unseen ball, at worse than
chance. The earlier law won pooled and the gate failed. The trade the skewness
family had shown reappeared in the excess family at about a third of the size and
with the same sign.

Before a ninth, an exploration on the rows already collected asked whether any shape
of the declared family holds both sides at once. Twelve configurations were scored
against the frozen law and none passed. The exploration named the mechanism instead.
Under an isotropic reader a uniform ball at 384 dimensions has a tenth-neighbour
concentration of 0.003, six times below a Gaussian's at the same dimension, the
law's concentration term is a reciprocal, it diverges there, and it predicts an
excess near zero against a truth of 1.4. Removing the reciprocal loses the real
corpora, which the reciprocal is what fits. That is a boundary of the family's shape
and not a missing world, and it is Definition 4 carried out on a declared complexity
ordering.

The ninth registered that boundary as the claim and searched nothing. A row is in
scope when its tenth-neighbour concentration is at least 0.015, a threshold declared
from the collected rows before the pilot and not moved after it. Inside the scope
the frozen law is claimed within the earlier bars on fresh worlds. Outside it the
law is not claimed, and its pooled error there must exceed the in-scope limit, or the
scope was a convenience and the claim fails as stated. That second clause is what
stops a scope from being a way of dropping the worlds that fail. The registration
also named in advance the one in-scope cell it expected to lose, a heavy-tailed
family at a sample size of 12000, with the reason, which is that the law carries no
term in sample size.

The run held every bar but that one. All eighteen unseen worlds sat inside the limit
on their in-scope rows, pooled 0.31 against a limit of 0.48, the real slices read
0.12 to 0.41, and the law beat both competitors in every group at 0.34 against 1.63
and 1.64. The 180 out-of-scope rows erred at 1.19 against 0.64, which is the second
clause holding. The named cell read 0.67 against 0.64. The verdict was
indeterminate, by the named cell and no other.

What stands is one law with one declared scope and two recorded boundaries. For a
corpus read through a linear observer, on rows whose tenth-neighbour distances vary
by at least 1.5 percent, the log of the busiest row's retrieval count over the
Poisson ceiling is a linear function of the reciprocal of that variation, the log of
the spectral dimension, and the square root of the intrinsic dimension of the
observed data, to a factor of about 1.4 in the excess, on every family of world the
line built, on corpus slices it never saw, and at four times its discovery sample.
Below 1.5 percent variation the concentration term diverges and the law is
withdrawn. For one heavy-tailed family the law under-predicts by about a factor of
three under the isotropic reader, and a sweep after the line closed showed this to be
a step in sample size between 4000 and 8000 followed by a plateau, which a law with
no term in sample size cannot follow. Neither boundary is repaired by a change of the
discovery set.

Three properties are visible in the record rather than asserted. The bars never
moved, and three registrations failed on clauses their own authors had written. Two
pilots froze a law that trailed a competitor on the pilot's own rows, and both were
sealed and run as registered with that fact recorded, because the transfer groups
are the test and the pilot is not. And when a registration that would pass was
wanted, the only route the record allowed was a change of claim, a boundary declared
before the run with its risk named, and the run returned the outcome the risk named.
The alternative route, dropping the worlds that fail without calling that a scope,
was available and was not taken.

The sequence of causes is the finding, and none of its five statements was available
at the first registration. The discovery set decides the law. The family cannot hold
both sides of the divide. The target was the limit. Coverage moves the error under
the new target too. The boundary is a divergent term and not a missing world. Each
was forced by a failure that had been reduced rather than absorbed.

\subsection{The check found a defect its own authors had not}

The preceding cases share a weakness a reader should hold against them. Every one is
the work of the people proposing the discipline, so each is a report of a method
applied by its authors to their own theories. The following episode is the nearest
thing to independent evidence the programme can currently offer, because in it the
method contradicted the people running it.

The four record types the protocol needs were implemented as machine-readable files,
among them a transformation registry declaring, per track, the admissible families
with the date each was declared. A checker was then written from Definition 1,
testing that every registered test names a family the registry carries. Run against
the programme's own registries for the first time, it reported ten tests, entered
across a single day of gate records, citing three families the registry did not
contain.

The finding was bookkeeping rather than a priority violation, and establishing which
took evidence rather than memory. Each of the three families was declared in its own
gate's sealed registration before the corresponding run, which is checkable in two
ways that do not rely on anybody's recollection. Each seal commit is an ancestor of
the commit that added that gate's results, and each sealed file still hashes to the
value the track document recorded. The declarations existed and preceded their
tests, and only the registry was late.

Two things about the episode bear on the argument. The first is that the defect had
been visible for two days in a published artifact and had not been seen. The
generator rendering each entry's invariance envelope prints a transformation
identifier followed by the sentence describing the family, so an undeclared
identifier printed an empty sentence, thirty times across four entries and the pages
built from them. Reading had not caught it. A check derived from a definition in
this paper caught it on first execution.

The second is what the repair had to look like. Adding the three families with the
current date would have made the registry pass and would have misdated the
declarations. Adding them with the declaration date alone would have concealed that
the registry was late. Deleting the offending tests would have removed the record of
tests that ran, which is the file drawer the discipline exists to prevent. The rows
were therefore entered carrying both dates and a pointer to the sealed artifact, so
the registry now says, in a form a stranger can check, that these three declarations
were made on one date and recorded on another. The specification was amended to
require exactly this, and to state the condition under which a late row is a repair
rather than a forgery, which is that the declaration it points at can be shown to
precede the result.

We report this because it is the only case here in which the discipline cost its
authors something, and because it illustrates the narrow claim of Section 2. No new
criterion of invariance was involved. What found the defect was the requirement that
the admissible class be a declared artifact rather than a sentence, and what made
the repair something other than cosmetic was the requirement that the record show
its own history.

\section{The fundamental structure conjecture}

The cases motivate a conjecture rather than a conclusion.

\begin{conjecture}[Fundamental Structure Conjecture]
Many apparently high-dimensional and domain-specific phenomena admit
low-complexity invariant structures acting on richer domain-specific
representations, and those structures can be progressively isolated by searching
for what survives admissible changes of representation, scale, population, and
task.
\end{conjecture}

The conjecture is broad and therefore easy to overstate, and three qualifications
are essential. Low dimensionality is not guaranteed, and some phenomena may be
irreducibly complex at the scale of interest, the cases in which ensembles beat
compact formulas being exactly the evidence a serious programme preserves, along
with the shape boundary of Section~\ref{sec:od} where the declared family had no
form holding both sides. Invariance is always relative to a transformation class, so
what survives translation may fail under a change of scale and what survives within
a culture may fail across cultures. And invariance does not imply ontology, being
evidence that a structure depends less on chosen descriptions rather than proof that
it is metaphysically ultimate.

The conjecture nonetheless supplies a search strategy. Begin with a rich
representation, identify a compact structure, transform the representation, transfer
the structure, and keep what survives. Repeated quotienting of representational
contingencies can expose an empirical core that becomes progressively harder to
remove without losing predictive power.

\section{Elegance, rigour, and the risk of beautiful error}

The discipline gives a precise role to two virtues. Elegance pushes toward fewer
primitives, reusable structures, and compression across apparently unrelated
phenomena. Rigour prevents elegance from becoming an aesthetic substitute for
evidence.

The danger is plain. A mathematically beautiful model can be wrong, and the more
beautiful it is the easier it is to rationalize away contrary evidence. Generating
hostile tests therefore belongs to the theory rather than to a later optional
exercise, and a mature research artifact should carry a claim ledger, a separation
of definitions from measured claims, a registry of admissible transformations,
preregistered gates, holdout data, ablations, and explicit statements of what would
force revision. Section~\ref{sec:od} shows what the last of those costs when it is
enforced, which is three failed registrations out of nine.

This is consistent with a pragmatist stance. The reality of a proposed structure is
not secured by metaphysical assertion. It earns standing by generating correct,
transferable predictions and by surviving increasingly severe attempts to remove
representational artifacts.

\section{A research programme}

Five workstreams follow.

\textbf{A calculus of philosophical invariants.} Develop a common formal language
for equivalence claims across epistemology, decision theory, ethics, philosophy of
mind, and philosophy of science. Each application specifies the representation
space, the output space, the transformation set, the output equivalence,
non-degeneracy criteria, uncertainty sets, and witness complexity. The immediate
deliverable is a taxonomy of invariance claims, which are exact, approximate,
equivariant, conditional, local, distributional, and scale-dependent.

\textbf{Representation attacks.} Construct benchmarks in which the same underlying
cases are presented through alternative coordinate systems, embeddings, languages,
schemas, and modalities, requiring candidate theories to survive invertible
transformations and to degrade predictably under lossy ones. This connects the
discipline to representation learning and symmetry discovery.

\textbf{Low-rank evaluation.} Test whether evaluator geometry inferred in one domain
transfers to another under independently determined representations. The decisive
experiments identify an evaluator in one domain, freeze the structure, and predict
choices in two others with minimal target-specific adjustment.

\textbf{Automated theory search.} Extend symbolic search from classification toward
general structure discovery with explicit complexity and invariance objectives, with
candidate outputs including equations, metric tensors, low-rank subspaces,
transformation groups, and boundary conditions, and with discovered structures
replayed on untouched test data.

\textbf{Boundary atlases.} Rather than publishing only the domains where a theory
works, build atlases of where invariances fail, estimating for each claimed symmetry
the boundary in scale, context, population, or representation at which the effect
becomes detectable. These maps may be worth more than a list of successes, because
they name the variables the next theory needs. The two boundaries of
Section~\ref{sec:od}, one in the concentration of a distance and one in sample size,
are entries in such an atlas, and each names a term a successor would have to carry.

\section{Objections}

\textbf{This is falsificationism with software.} The discipline is compatible with
falsificationist virtues and is not exhausted by binary refutation. Its distinctive
object is the structure of the counterexample, and minimal witnesses, transformation
grammars, and boundary estimates turn a failed prediction into information about
which claimed equivalence stopped holding. The method is closer to debugging a
formal architecture than to recording a rejected hypothesis \citep{zeller2002}.

\textbf{Invariance is not truth.} Correct. A false theory can be invariant and a
true local theory can legitimately fail outside its domain.
\citet{vanfraassen1989} presses the stronger version, that symmetry arguments carry
substantive commitments about what counts as a difference, and the present account
accepts it by treating the transformation class as declared and contestable rather
than discovered. Invariance is therefore comparative evidence against
representational arbitrariness and not a truth criterion, and predictive adequacy,
independent replication, and domain specification remain necessary.

\textbf{The transformation class is chosen by the theorist.} Also correct, and it is
the central governance problem of any invariance-based method, the one
\citet{nozick2001} raises without settling and the one the robustness literature
treats as its main risk. The remedy is not to pretend the choice is objective. It is
to make it explicit, versioned, contestable, and empirically revisable, so competing
philosophical positions appear as competing transformation registries and the
disagreement is located rather than argued. In the programmes reported here the
registry is a file, it is sealed before the runs that test it, and
Section~\ref{sec:od} is a case in which the declared family and not the data turned
out to be the limit.

\textbf{Philosophical claims are often normative.} Normative claims are not settled
by observing behaviour, and the discipline does not infer an ought from an is. It
tests the operational consequences of a normative principle once the principle is
formalized, which means whether an implementation respects declared equivalences,
whether a proposed standard is internally invariant, whether a policy rule produces
representation-dependent outcomes, and whether a normative model predicts human
judgments when offered as a descriptive theory.

\textbf{Search will overfit.} Automated search creates severe multiplicity and
researcher degrees of freedom, so the discipline requires separation of discovery
from evaluation, fair holdout replay, preregistration where feasible, and
independent replication. An expression found after searching millions of candidates
is not evidence until its search exposure is accounted for and its performance
survives untouched data. Section~\ref{sec:od} is the worked case, the search having
run nine times over one declared family with every bar a multiple of a pilot error
fixed before the seal, and the two gates whose frozen law trailed a competitor on
the pilot's own rows run as registered rather than re-searched.

\textbf{The evidence is the proponents' own work.} This is the objection we think
strongest. Every case in Section 7 comes from one programme, and a method applied by
its authors to their own theories is not independently tested, however sealed its
registrations. Three things mitigate and none removes it. The registrations, data,
graders, and run logs are public, so the verdicts are checkable even though they are
not independent. The record retains outcomes that embarrass the programme, among
them three failed registrations, two indeterminate gates, a published invariance
that failed on external data, and a population in which a deterministic theorem
holds for half the respondents. And Section 7.6 is a case in which the discipline's
own check overruled its authors. What is missing is a second programme, run by other
people, and until that exists the claims here are claims about a method demonstrated
once.

\section{Engineering as an epistemic instrument}

The phrase philosophy engineering can suggest that philosophy supplies requirements
and engineering implements them. The relationship proposed here is stronger.
Engineering exposes the hidden commitments of a philosophy by forcing them into
interfaces, data structures, registries, test cases, and failure modes, and
ambiguities surviving prose become hard to ignore when a system must decide whether
two cases belong to the same equivalence class.

This changes the epistemic role of implementation. A program is not only a
demonstration that a theory can be instantiated. It can be a measurement instrument
for the theory. Canonicalizers test claims about sameness. Equivariant architectures
test claims about symmetry. Symbolic search tests claims about compressibility.
Cross-domain transfer tests claims about generality. Minimal witnesses test claims
about irrelevance. Boundary maps test claims about scope.

The resulting practice is neither armchair philosophy nor ordinary empirical
science. It is a disciplined traffic between conceptual analysis and experiment.
Philosophy proposes distinctions, mathematics makes them precise, engineering makes
them executable, experiment pressures them, and philosophy must then explain the
returns.

The operational sense of fundamental that follows is modest. Whatever survives
repeated removal of representational contingencies, while preserving discriminative
and predictive adequacy, is a better candidate for fundamental structure than what
disappears under harmless redescription. The search can fail, and may reveal no
compact invariant core. That too is a substantive result.

\section{Conclusion}

The idea that what survives admissible transformation is a better candidate for the
real has been stated four times already, in geometry, in robustness analysis, in the
theory of causal explanation, and in structural realism. This paper does not improve
on those statements. It argues that the idea has been held in a form that cannot be
audited, and that the three things a prose statement leaves in the claimant's hands,
the admissible class, the reduction of the failure, and the revision, can be made
into declared artifacts a checker refuses when they are absent.

What follows is a discipline whose methodology is six verbs. Formalize, derive,
search, probe, witness, revise. We have reported it running on four graded
predictions of one theory, on a published invariance that failed on independent
data, and on a discovery line that asked one question nine times and ended with one
law, one declared scope, and two boundaries. We have also reported the case in which
the discipline's own check found a defect in the records of the people who wrote it,
which is the only evidence here not resting on our good faith.

The deepest promise is not that philosophy is replaced by engineering, nor that
every profound question has a computational answer. It is that some philosophical
propositions can be made precise enough to become experimentally vulnerable. Once
that happens, implementation stops being application and becomes inquiry.

The guiding question is therefore not whether the fundamental theory has been found.
It is more disciplined. What remains after every distinction that should not matter
has been transformed away, every compact structure has been attacked on unseen data,
and every failure has been reduced to the smallest witness available? Whatever
survives that has earned the right to be taken seriously as fundamental.
